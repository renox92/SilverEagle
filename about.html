About this site

This page explains what each number on the Muster Roll actually measures, and how the boss records are picked — for anyone who plays with us, and as a standing note-to-self so the method doesn't quietly drift from what this page says it is.

The 21-day window

The muster roll and the rhythm strip both look at the same 21 days — three full calendar weeks, Monday through Sunday, ending on the Sunday of the current week. That window shifts forward a week at a time, on Mondays.

A day only enters the numbers once it has actually been scanned and committed. A day with no screenshot yet is shown as not captured, which is different from a day someone simply didn't play — the two are never counted the same way, and neither counts as a zero score when working out anyone's average.

Muster roll columns

ColumnWhat it measures
TOTALSum of a player's scores across the days they played within the 21-day window. Days not played or not yet captured simply aren't in the sum.
AVG / DAYThe mean — total divided by the number of days actually played, not by 21. A quiet week where someone missed a few days doesn't drag this down; it only reflects the days they showed up for.
DAYS PLAYEDHow many of the days that were actually captured this player has a score for — the second number is the count of captured days, not 21.
RHYTHMOne cell per day. Colour reflects that player's share of that single day's guild total — it's a relative comparison to everyone else that day, not a fixed scale.
SHAREThis player's total as a percentage of everyone's combined total for the same window.
#Rank by TOTAL only. Players who have left the guild are shown at the bottom with no rank — their history stays visible, but they're not compared against people still playing.

A player's own page

Click a name to open their detail view. The headline number there is different from the table on purpose:

FieldWhat it measures
Median per day playedThe median, not the mean, of the same played-day scores as AVG / DAY. This is the deliberate headline figure: a single huge outlier day, or a player who has only played a handful of times, can pull a mean a long way from what a "typical" day for them actually looks like — the median resists that. If the mean disagrees with the median by more than 15%, it's shown alongside as a footnote so the gap isn't hidden, but the median is what's meant to represent them.
Average by weekdayThis chart is a mean — a player's average score on each weekday, counting only the weekdays they've actually played.
Best dayTheir single highest score recorded in the window.

Boss of the day

Each boss tile shows two different figures, and they're measured very differently from everything above:

The median on the tile

This is the all-time median of every individual score anyone has ever recorded on that weekday — not the 21-day window, the whole history of committed days. It also isn't limited to current members: a score from someone who has since left the guild still counts toward it, the same as everyone else's.

The top 3 (hover to see them)

This is not simply the three highest scores ever logged on that weekday — that could easily be one player's three best outings, crowding out everyone else. Instead, each player's own single best score ever on that weekday is found first, and those personal-best figures are ranked against each other. The top 3 are shown, which guarantees three different people, each represented by their own personal best — along with the date each of those three records was set. The line at the bottom of the popup (e.g. "112 scores over 32 Mondays") is the sample size behind the median above it: how many qualifying scores, across how many distinct days, feed that weekday's number.

Also all-time, also not limited to current members — someone who left the guild can still be sitting in a boss record if that score is still one of the top 3 ever set on that weekday.
